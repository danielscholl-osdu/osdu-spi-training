# Fieldnote 02 · How it comes to life

**Status: v2 published.** v1 was cut from lesson 02 (`chapters['bring-up']`) and the take recorded on 12 October 2026, which stalled at 20 of 29; it runs 2 minutes 50 seconds, and its cut-from record is in `src/content/videos.js`. v2 is cut from the 22 October take on spi 0.32.0, which reached Ready and a successful lookup. Claim headlines, the example, and the myth are unchanged, so the cut-from record keeps its claims; only claim 2's last sentences, its board, and the take record change.

**Who is watching:** an engineer who has seen lesson 01 and is about to create an environment. **What they should believe after it:** `spi up` hands off to Flux rather than finishing the job; a returned command is not a usable API; and `spi down` keeps the names and identities while the data goes.

**The form is mixed: boards and real terminal footage.** The footage is one dated take of a fresh `spi up` into a throwaway environment, its readiness checks, and its `spi down`, recorded as described under _The take_ below. Footage shows what happened; boards explain it. A time on screen is either measured in the take, with its clock shown, or labeled illustrative.

**The scene:** boards are concept scenes in vector motion, in the site's colors and fonts. Footage sits in a terminal pane on the sheet, cropped to the lines that matter and zoomed to read at phone width, never a full-screen screenshot. Owner colors follow lesson 07's legend: CLI + Bicep, Flux, controllers. One generated illustration carries the recall. Everything stays at lesson 02's rung: the resource group, the cluster, and its namespaces as rooms; services are opaque boxes.

One continuous session, recorded with its timing so every on-screen time comes from the recording's clock. It runs in a throwaway environment in the developer subscription, from the CLI release current on the day, against the stack's `main` branch, in the default region.

- **Footage record:** the cut's `NOTES.md` keeps the CLI release, the stack commit Flux verified, the region, the shell, the date, and the measured times (CLI exit, Ready, Jobs Complete, first successful lookup, `spi down` duration). - The resource group is purged after the take, so nothing is left billing.

**What this take recorded.** spi 0.32.0, stack at release tag `v0.32.0` (`3d3106d`), core profile, `westus3`, zsh on macOS, 22 October 2026 (UTC). `spi up` started 22:16:19Z and returned success after 44 minutes 0 seconds; the Git source artifact was stored at +42:13, 1 minute 47 seconds before the CLI returned, with `spec.suspend: true`. Kustomizations went from 2 of 30 to 30 of 30, holding longest at 22; `spi status --json` first reported Ready at +1:07:10. The partition lookup returned `HTTP 200` at +1:08:16. The stack left the environment in maintenance (not Deployable) until the operator cleared it, which is lesson 10's subject and stays out of this episode. `spi down --env <name> --force` started at 00:13:10Z on 23 October and returned after 14 minutes 58 seconds; `az resource list` then showed only the five managed identities (`ctl-id`, `member`, `deployer`, `noaccess`, `osdu-identity`) in the resource group. The purge followed off camera, and `az group exists` returned `false` at 00:29:48Z. The teardown footage shows the subscription id in `az resource delete` lines; it is masked in the cut. The 12 October take (spi 0.28.1, stalled at 20 of 29 on a community image lineage the stack retired in 0.30) is kept in the video library as v1's record.

## Word list the narration must keep

- `spi up` and `spi down` are spoken "S P I up" and "S P I down"; CLI is "C L I".
- Flux starts before the CLI exits. There is no single moment when the CLI stops and Flux starts; the lanes overlap, and the board never draws a handoff line between them.
- `spi status --watch` follows rollout; it makes no API call. Only an authenticated request verifies an API path, and only the path it exercised.
- Teardown keeps the managed identities and the resource group, not the data. Kubernetes seed Secrets go with the cluster, so a rebuild gets new middleware passwords; the narration says "names", never "the same environment".
- Suspension stops fetching new commits. It does not stop Flux applying the cached revision or controllers running.
- The take's times are said once, as what this take measured. Never say the stack usually takes that long.
- Measured times belong to this take only and are labeled as such. Never add overlapping phase times into a total.
- Profiles, chart Secrets, and the CI teardown job are other stages' mistakes and stay out of this episode.
