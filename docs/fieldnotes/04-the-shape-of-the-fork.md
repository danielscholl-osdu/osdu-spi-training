# Fieldnote 04 · The shape of the fork

**Status: in production.** Cut from lesson 04 (`chapters['fork-shape']`). Aim for about 2 minutes 50 seconds.

**Who is watching:** an engineer who has just seen the Azure provider inside the partition service in Fieldnote 03. **What they should believe after it:** the fork owns a short list of paths and everything else is regenerated from upstream every day; three branches have three jobs; and the generated branch never carries the Azure paths, so an upstream deletion has nothing to delete.

**The form is mixed: boards and GitHub footage.** Footage is captured from the public pages of `Azure/osdu-spi-partition` on the day of the cut: the branch list, the `provider/` folder on two branches, and the generated commit's trailers. Boards explain the ownership grid the footage can only hint at. Lesson 04's outcomes are plain sentences, so its chapters are titled with the sentences, as the site does today; per-claim cues arrive when the lesson moves to the claim grammar.

**The scene:** the lesson's figure, "One repository, read by owner", built as a vector grid: paths as rows, the three branches as columns, cells that say where each path exists. Fork-owned rows are orange; upstream's rows are ink. Upstream sits outside the grid. Footage sits in a browser pane on the sheet, cropped to the element that matters, never a full-page screenshot. One generated illustration carries the recall.

## Word list the narration must keep

- `fork_upstream` and `fork_integration` are spoken "fork upstream" and "fork integration".
- The example is illustrative from its first hop: the real cache fix arrived from upstream before the filter existed. The narration says so once, at the start.
- The fork owns paths, not the repository. Never say the fork "owns the code" without the path.
- Upstream _plans_ to remove its Azure code. Never say it has.
- `osdu-spi-partition` is the reference fork and, as of September 2026, the only one. The episode does not mention the other seven.
- Mirror forks and the sync and cascade mechanics belong to Fieldnote 05.
