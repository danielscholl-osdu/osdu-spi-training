# Fieldnote S202 · Who is asking

**Status: published.** Level 200, the second episode of the service path. Its companion page is `chapters['who-is-asking']`, and its cut-from record is in `src/content/videos.js`; the curriculum entry is `S202` in `src/content/curriculum.js` (teaches `mesh-and-entitlements` and `role-is-a-preset`, uses `one-gateway`, home of the myth `users-add-accumulates`).

**Who is watching:** an engineer on the service path with a Deployable environment of their own (S201) who has so far called the APIs with `spi token`. **What they should believe after it:** their own Azure token gets through the mesh and is then refused by entitlements until they are a member; `spi users add --me` makes them one and proves it with their own token; and a role is one preset of groups, set rather than added to.

**The scene:** the body is Fieldnote 09's: the take's own terminal panes, masked, beside boards that draw what the terminal cannot show. Owners: the `spi` CLI, Flux for the mesh and the sidecar's identity filter, OSDU services in neutral ink, and you. Group names in code type, never in an owner color. No orange: no fork code changes. Level 200: real commands and their output, the usual path, and one failure (the 401); the claim order the mesh applies and the identities bring-up seeds stay below this level.

**Made from:** Fieldnote 09 (22 October take on spi 0.32.0). The cold open (c1) and its take are kept as cut. The standfirst (n2) and the traced example (n6, which retold n3 and n4 and the cold open's flip) are dropped. n3, n4, n5 and n7 keep their boards and footage with the cuts below and are re-voiced; n0, n1, n8 and n9 are new takes.

## The companion page

The page `who-is-asking`, rewritten from this storyboard:

- Question: "Who does the stack think you are?"
- Claim 1, "The mesh authenticates and entitlements authorizes": "spi token --me prints your own Azure token; the mesh accepts it and writes your unique_name as the caller, entitlements refuses you with 401 until you are a member, and spi users add --me makes you one and checks with your own token."
- Claim 2, "A role is a preset of groups, and spi users add replaces it": "A role is one of four presets of groups, and you hold one at a time: viewer is users and users.datalake.viewers; editor, admin and ops each add their own groups and users.datalake.impersonation; and spi users add with another role replaces the one you held."
- Example: the page keeps _From 401 to 200_, `spi users add --me --role viewer`, five hops from your token to entitlements' 200. The episode traces no example; n4 shows the same flip inside the first chapter.
- Easy mistake: "Running spi users add again adds to the role I hold." (`users-add-accumulates`, checked with `spi users list`).
- The seeded identities, the mesh's claim order, and what impersonation lets the stack do stay off the claims; the drawer keeps the checks.

## Keep it true

- A person's v1 `az` token passes the mesh because the v1 issuer rule accepts the management audience (ADR-016); the identity filter on each service's sidecar writes `x-user-id` from `unique_name`. Never say the mesh looks you up or authorizes you; Entitlements is the only box that says yes or no.
- The gateway is named in a clause, as the stack's one way in; no board draws the request path.
- Plain `spi token` mints the deploy identity (`token.py`, `caller: str = "deploy"`); `--me` prints the person's own token. Never call `spi token` "your token".
- `spi users add --me` stores the id the filter projects and verifies by calling entitlements with the person's token for up to 60 seconds (`VERIFY_TIMEOUT`). It always passes `--role` on screen; the default role without it is `admin`.
- The four presets are `ROLE_PRESETS` in `src/spi/users.py`. Editor, admin and ops carry `users.datalake.impersonation`; viewer does not. What impersonation does, and ingestion as the caller, is S203's; this episode gives it one forward clause, and no line speaks an episode code.
- A role is set, not accumulated: `add` with another role removes the other presets' groups, then adds this one's; `users` stays.
- Do not explain the group counts (`18`, `26`) the footage prints.
